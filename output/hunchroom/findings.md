# Shadow Futures: finite comparison-budget impossibility

Requested contribution: a Lean proof and independent review of the correspondence between the formal statement and Theorem 1 of Martin Erlic's *Shadow Futures: Contribution Uncertainty and the Self-Reinforcing Market* (December 2025; revised July 2026), particularly Section 3 (pp. 6-7) and Appendix B (pp. 17-19).

## Mathematical request

Consider one adaptive allocation market with a fixed finite recipient set of size n >= 2. Its only random observations are the recipients. The complete record is an infinite recipient sequence. At each finite history h, a parameter beta determines strictly positive normalized next-recipient probabilities p(beta,h,i). A maximizing recipient w(beta,h) determines residual contestability epsilon(beta,h) = 1 - p(beta,h,w(beta,h)).

Suppose the squared Hellinger distance between any pair of conditional recipient laws is bounded at every history by a finite parameter-pair constant times residual contestability under the first law. The quantification over ordered parameter pairs imposes the reverse bound too. Suppose the comparison budget, the sum of residual contestability along a history, is finite almost surely under every parameter's own path law.

Prove these four conclusions:

1. Every pair of complete-history laws is mutually absolutely continuous.
2. For any nonconstant scalar contribution functional F(beta), no estimator using only the finite observed prefix converges in probability to F(beta) at every beta.
3. No sequence of finite-prefix binary tests has both type-I and type-II error probabilities tending to zero.
4. No finite-prefix confidence procedure for a nonconstant scalar F has coverage tending to one at every beta while its diameter tends to zero in probability at every beta.

This is a canonical finite-recipient specialization of the paper's theorem. It fixes a common deterministic initial state and has no extra parameter-dependent observations. Strict positivity is stronger than the paper's local-equivalence condition and covers the strictly positive conditional-logit model. The theorem allows arbitrary adaptive prefix kernels, so it is not restricted to polynomial reinforcement or eventual allocation monopoly.

## Correspondence with Statement.lean

- `C h` is exactly the cylinder event consisting of complete sequences beginning with h. The measurable space is generated by those cylinders, fixing the raw terminal information set.
- `P beta Set.univ = 1` makes every P beta a probability law. The cylinder recursion fixes its conditional recipient probabilities, including the common empty initial history.
- `p` contains the next-recipient probabilities. The sum of squared differences of `Real.sqrt` values is exactly the paper's squared Hellinger distance.
- `w` selects a recipient maximizing p, and `1 - p beta h (w beta h)` is the paper's residual contestability. Normalization and positivity make this nonnegative.
- The zero-measure event of failure of `Summable` encodes a finite comparison budget almost surely. It is imposed separately under each law, without imposing a finite expected budget.
- Ordered parameter-pair Hellinger bounds impose both directions. Mutual absolute continuity is a conclusion, not an assumption.
- Estimators, tests and confidence sets are functions of date and finite prefix. Their error-event probabilities tend to zero in the extended nonnegative reals.
- Confidence width is expressed using the event that the set contains two points separated by at least epsilon. Requiring those event probabilities to tend to zero for every positive epsilon is equivalent to diameter shrinking in probability; empty sets are permitted, but the coverage condition excludes an always-empty procedure.
- Both contribution functionals are real valued. The paper's more general metric-valued version is outside this target.

## Argument supplied by the preprint

The comparison bound and almost-sure finite budget imply a finite accumulated conditional Hellinger process. Local equivalence and the discrete-time filtered-space Hellinger criterion then give absolute continuity on the generated terminal sigma-field. Reversing the ordered parameter pair gives mutual absolute continuity.

For estimation, absolute continuity between fixed finite measures transfers probabilities of measurable events tending to zero. A proposed estimator consistent under one parameter must therefore converge to the same constant under another; it cannot converge to two different contribution values under that second law. The testing and shrinking-confidence conclusions use the same transfer of vanishing event probabilities.

An independent mathematical review of the paper's discrete-time specialization found the criterion's direction and normalization consistent with Gabriyelyan's primary result. This review is not a Lean proof, not a Hunchroom meaning-review verdict, and not proof verification.

## Economic interpretation and limits

Productive inputs may truly affect every allocation probability. The claimed limit concerns recovering their contribution from one endogenous market history when total comparison is finite. Transaction count alone is not the comparison budget.

Mutual absolute continuity means the laws share null events. It does not mean identical distributions, no finite-sample information, or no causal effect of productive inputs. Increasing returns, concentration or path dependence alone do not establish the hypotheses. A finite simulation cannot verify the infinite-history budget condition. Independent replications and additional observed channels may provide information outside this target.

The conditional-logit equations, their comparison-dominated Hellinger bound, the Fisher-information inequality, strong-reinforcement absorption, latent-position gauge, finite-horizon rates, taxation, policy prescriptions and empirical classification of real markets are separate proof or economic interpretation obligations. They are not certified by proving this target.

The paper already gives a mathematical argument. This contribution requests its formal verification in the stated specialization. It does not assert a new proof, an established economics breakthrough, or verified novelty. The website illustrates and explains the argument and is included as context, not as proof evidence.
