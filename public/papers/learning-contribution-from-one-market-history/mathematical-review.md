## Theorem 2

1. **Clock construction and rate.** After seller $i$ has won $k$ awards, its next clock has rate $c_i(k+1)^\alpha$. Memorylessness gives the purchase odds in §5. For $\lambda=1-\alpha$ and $0<d<\min(1/2,\lambda)$, the normalized centered clock increments have variances proportional to $j^{-2+2d}$, a summable sequence. Kronecker's lemma gives the asserted little-o remainder. The deterministic sum differs from $k^\lambda/\lambda$ by a bounded amount. Each clock diverges and both counts grow without bound. The adjacent-time bounds hold simultaneously for all large integer counts, allowing substitution of the random counts. Their leading terms first give the limiting count ratio, then their remainders give $z_n-\mu=o(n^{-d})$ by inversion about the interior root.
2. **Accumulated variation.** I expanded the one-step square in D.2 from the smoothed-share recursion. On the proposed finite-energy event, the drift terms are absolutely summable and the cross term is a martingale with finite quadratic variation after localization. Centering the last squared increment leaves a convergent martingale series. Its predictable part sums to $[s(1-s)+o(1)]\log N$. Telescoping would therefore force squared deviations eventually above a positive multiple of $\log N/N$, contradicting the proposed event. The logit transformation preserves divergence near the interior limit. Prefix truncation at any fixed length justifies the additional claim about dispersion around the empirical mean.
3. **Estimation with the true and fitted anchors.** The true-anchor score divided by its accumulated squared regressor converges to zero by predictable normalization. Boundedness of the converging log-odds path gives a strictly positive path-dependent lower bound on logistic curvature for every candidate slope in $[0,1]$. This yields uniform likelihood separation outside each fixed neighborhood of the truth. The terminal anchor changes each prefix log likelihood by at most $m_T|z_T-\mu|=o(1)$. This bound holds pathwise even though the data overlap. Substitution recovers the intercept.
4. **Counterexample attempts.** At zero quality, deterministic alternating awards produce square-summable deviations. That infinite path has probability zero under the model, since conditional win probabilities along it approach one half. With zero reinforcement the outcomes are independent and the random past still supplies a slope contrast. At the critical half-power the variance bound in D.1 holds for every strict $d<1/2$. Arbitrarily near the linear boundary a positive $d<1-\alpha$ still exists at each fixed parameter. The proof supplies neither uniform accuracy near that boundary nor a slope convergence rate. An unrestricted seller intercept confounds quality exactly, as §9 acknowledges.

## Priority and scope of the contribution

- Maldonado, Van Hentenryck, Berbeglia and Berbeglia give purchase probabilities proportional to visibility times quality times a power of purchases. With two goods, fixed equal visibility and relative quality $e^\beta$, their equations (1) and (5) give the paper's rule. Equation (11) and Theorem 5.3 give the same interior limit for $0<\alpha<1$. Credit the model and market-share equilibrium explicitly. [Maldonado et al., §§3–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.07251)
- Pham, Sheridan and Shimodaira jointly estimate attachment and node fitness using penalized likelihood for a growing network. They establish a direct precedent for the joint estimation objective. Here the choice set stays fixed at two sellers and the estimator exploits departures from one limiting share. [Pham et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep32558)
- There is also a direct statistical connection. With $F(x)=\sigma(\beta+\alpha\operatorname{logit}x)$, differentiation at the equilibrium gives $F'(s)=\alpha$. The stochastic-approximation drift $x-F(x)$ therefore has root $s$ and slope $1-\alpha$. Lai and Robbins studied slope estimation along convergent stochastic-approximation designs. Gerencsér's Theorem 1.1 treats a critical continuous-time linear process with Brownian noise. Whether a general earlier theorem directly implies the entire discrete Bernoulli result remains unresolved. [Lai and Robbins, 1981](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00536178) · [Lai's account of the assumptions, §2](https://tzelai.ckirby.su.domains/pubs/2021_StochApprox.pdf) · [Gerencsér, 1995](https://doi.org/10.1137/1140011)
- Le Goff and Soulier's attractiveness parameter is a common additive initial count. Their Theorem 8(ii) gives a first-order limit for the normalized likelihood at the sublinear truth. That limit alone cannot decide separation on a slower scale. Retain the distinction between their parameter and multiplicative relative fitness. [Le Goff and Soulier, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2322)

The precise claim supported here is an explicit estimator and self-contained consistency proof for a fixed pair from one ordered award history throughout the sublinear model. Finite-horizon uncertainty, the full-history joint MLE and the linear boundary require separate results.
