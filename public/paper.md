## Abstract

A market can observe product quality perfectly, record every sale, and still exhaust the comparisons needed to learn how much quality drives sales. When past sales shape future exposure, I measure the remaining scope for comparison by summing, sale by sale, the probabilities that someone other than the current favorite wins. In a conditional logit model with bounded input differences, this comparison budget bounds Fisher information about the return to quality. If the lifetime budget is almost surely finite at every parameter value, no estimator using one history can consistently recover a nonconstant measure of contribution across those values. Models that assign different effects to quality nevertheless converge in their forecasts of the entire future sales path. An AI distributor can exercise epistemic monopoly by deciding which rival models get the deployments that would put its own lead to the test. Taxation faces a separate limit. If two economies generate the same law for observable histories but assign different positional rents to the same reward, no tax based on the record can collect the exact rent in both.

## Central result

The comparison budget is cumulative probability mass remaining outside the currently dominant alternative. Under the paper's common-design, local-equivalence, Hellinger-control, and finite-comparison conditions, distinct contribution parameters generate mutually absolutely continuous complete-history laws.

The implication is an identification limit. No estimator based on one realized market can consistently recover every nonconstant contribution functional, and no test can separate two contribution parameters with vanishing total error.

Strong reinforcement is one sharp corollary because it can exhaust the comparison budget and produce eventual allocation monopoly. With latent position, contribution and position can be exactly observationally equivalent.
