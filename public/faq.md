# Shadow Futures FAQ

Canonical page: https://shadow-futures.vercel.app/faq

## What are shadow futures?

Shadow futures are unrealized, positive-probability market histories with the same productive inputs, rules, and initial state but different allocation shocks and terminal rewards. They are the alternative paths needed to distinguish contribution from position and reinforcement.

## Does the theory say work, talent, quality, or risk do not matter?

No. These inputs can be real, observed, and causally affect every reward probability. The result is about identification: their contribution may still be unrecoverable from the one path that rewarded them.

## Why is a large transaction count not enough?

When one alternative is almost certain to win, later transactions mostly repeat its lead. Transaction volume can grow while the probability of a meaningful comparison approaches zero.

## What is the comparison budget?

It is the cumulative probability that the next allocation could go to someone other than the current favorite. It tracks the market's remaining supply of competitive comparison.

## Is this just preferential attachment or increasing returns?

No. Preferential attachment and increasing returns describe mechanisms by which leads compound. Shadow Futures studies what the resulting loss of comparison means for identifying contribution.

## Does inequality prove the theorem?

No. A Lorenz curve or final market-share distribution describes the outcome. It does not by itself identify the causal contribution of productive inputs, position, reinforcement, or allocation shocks.

## What can preserve learning?

Meaningful randomized exposure, independent starts, portability, multihoming, open standards, structural separation, independent procurement, and public options can create genuinely different paths. These are interventions that alter the comparison process.

## What does the result imply for taxation and redistribution?

It does not calculate an individual's exact luck or deserved share. It shows why a reinforced market reward is not a precise certificate of contribution. Tax rates, UBI, social dividends, and public ownership remain collective choices that should be justified directly.

## Does this apply to AI agents and automated payments?

Potentially. A high volume of machine-to-machine payments can still extend one reinforced purchasing path rather than create independent tests of competing agents or firms. The relevant question is whether the system preserves meaningful alternative allocations.
