# Bounded book writing

Produce a reviewable writing result without turning each request into another whole-book research project. Follow the author's scope, voice, evidence standard, and word allowance. Efficiency must not conceal missing work or weaken a substantive claim without explanation.

## Set the pass

State the concrete deliverable, affected chapters or issue list, and stopping point in one short update. Use the latest handoff and current files to choose the next useful work. If no issue list exists, address recorded open issues and recent changes. Do not create an open-ended goal, new chapter, export format, or additional review phase unless requested.

Default to one chapter or one specified cross-chapter revision per pass. A larger authorized assignment can have several bounded phases; retain its full scope and distinguish a completed phase from a completed book. Follow explicit token/time/source limits. Do not invent a numerical spending guarantee.

## Read selectively

- Read applicable repository instructions once, then the relevant outline/brief passages, affected prose, and its source notes. Reuse unchanged material already read in the conversation.
- Use headings, search, and bounded excerpts before dumping files. For continuity, read the changed passage and its neighbors. Read the entire book only for an explicitly scoped whole-book pass, once.
- Reuse claim-specific source checks when the claim, source version, and relevant text are unchanged. A citation inventory is weaker evidence than an actual recorded passage check. A changed generated reader does not invalidate every chapter's previous review.
- Let scripts count words, match notes, and compare hashes. Read their concise results, not the entire generated manuscript or every JSON record after each edit.

## Write and review

Draft toward the chapter's narrative job: a concrete case or question, explanation, evidence, implications, and a useful transition. Preserve documented facts, predecessor credit, and necessary conceptual distinctions. Distinguish reporting, interpretation, hypothetical examples, and policy proposals. Invent no reporting or quotations. Honor the author's mathematical and technical level.

For revision, fix the stated problems and material errors encountered in affected text. Do not rewrite sound adjacent chapters merely for stylistic uniformity. Keep both main text and back matter within their allowances; avoid padding to hit an exact count.

Do one editorial read of the changed text for accessibility, argument, repetition, and transitions. Carry remaining optional improvements into the issue list instead of starting another polishing cycle. Reopen a passed check only after a relevant change or new evidence.

## Research with a stop rule

Research new, changed, unsupported, or time-sensitive consequential claims. Start with the existing ledger and the strongest relevant primary source. Record the passage/version and what it supports. Do not repeat a completed literature review.

For an inaccessible work, make one direct attempt and at most one credible alternate access attempt in this pass. Consult the retry history first; do not repeat previously failed routes without new evidence. Stop on the access limit, retain a precise unresolved flag, and continue independent work. Never imply an unread source was checked. Budget a difficult source or proof audit as a separate task when needed; do not silently replace required verification with weaker evidence.

## Close once

Run the established assembly and integrity checks once after the edits. Correct an actual failure and rerun only affected checks. Do not build a second audit framework or new test suite for ordinary prose edits. Mechanical checks do not certify originality, proofs, factual completeness, or publication readiness.

Update one concise handoff or review record with the affected files/version, outcome, unresolved claims, and next action. Reconcile stale completion labels where they would mislead. Save or commit using the project's established workflow and existing authorization.

Report what changed, how it was checked, the material remaining issues, and the checkpoint. Then stop at the agreed boundary. Do not automatically launch the next phase or repeat status turns. A short, explicit unfinished-issues list is more useful than an indefinite verification loop.
