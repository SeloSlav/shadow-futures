## The argument to develop

An economy can contain many active agents while having few independent authorities over investment, distribution, and evaluation. The important questions concern who owns the resulting assets, who can redirect the work, which experiments receive resources, and whether outsiders can obtain an interpretable test. The number of agents, firms, model brands, and transactions cannot answer those questions by itself.

This creates a conditional political-economic possibility: public action limiting a gatekeeper's control over access could preserve more room for private enterprise than a policy that leaves that particular bottleneck untouched. The same public power could instead protect a national champion, impose one official evaluation, or prevent an unwelcome result from being published. The proposed institutional advantage belongs to arrangements that preserve useful, revisable discovery while sustaining production. It does not belong automatically to a continent, political system, or degree of government intervention.

## Begin with sixteen agents that needed another test

Use Nicholas Carlini's February 5, 2026 account at Anthropic as the principal new production case. He reports a team of sixteen Claude instances working on a C compiler. When they attempted the Linux build, agents encountered the same bug and interfered with one another's fixes. His revised harness used GCC to compile most files, leaving varying subsets to the new compiler. That made different failures more separable. The resulting system still had substantial limitations, including reliance on other tools and inefficient generated code. Attribute the account throughout: this is a developer's capability experiment, not an independently audited comparison of firms, employment, or total production costs. [Original account](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler).

The interpretive move is concrete. Another running agent did not solve the coordination problem until the environment supplied more useful feedback. Equally, the episode demonstrates a real route to learning inside one company with related models. Shared ownership or model ancestry does not erase an informative physical or software test. The book must retain both implications.

Then move to a labeled Birch thought experiment. A small engineering firm buys agent services to investigate defects, write changes, and run checks. Under one contract, it can retain its code, tests, records, and customers and move to another provider. Under another, essential access and usable project history remain tied to one supplier. The first arrangement could make entry cheaper; the second could make a growing business increasingly dependent. These are stipulated alternatives, not descriptions of current vendor terms.

## Capital that can perform work

Follow the same fictional firm's balance sheet and working day. Money buys temporary model access, owned equipment, staff supervision, customer acquisition, and perhaps durable tools. These are different claims on production. Renting more agents is an operating expense; profits become accumulated capital only when saved and invested in assets or capabilities. Productivity, receipts, profit, wealth, and bargaining power must not slide into one another.

Set out two possible feedback processes. In one, cheaper software work helps new entrants build products, run tests, and finance the next experiment. In the other, an established owner reinvests returns in infrastructure, privileged access, and distribution, making the next entrant's first credible trial harder to obtain. Neither process follows from the mere existence of an agent swarm. Competition, depreciation, copying, financing, physical bottlenecks, demand, and productive benefits can change the result.

Credit Anton Korinek and Donghyun Suh's March 2024 *Scenarios for the Transition to AGI*. Their model studies automation and capital accumulation together; wage outcomes depend on task complexity and other assumptions. Its extensions distinguish reproducible capital from scarce fixed factors. Use this as established scenario analysis, not a forecast of wage collapse or a theorem supplied by Shadow Futures. The additional question here is which owners and institutions retain access to valuable comparison as production changes. [Author paper](https://cdn.governance.ai/AGI_Scenarios.pdf), especially the introduction, Section 3, Section 4.1, and conclusion.

Ownership then becomes a serious institutional choice: compare a worker who rents an agent service, a worker with a capital stake, a cooperative buying shared infrastructure, and a public facility supplying qualified access. None guarantees either broad gains or useful discovery. A widely owned asset can still depend on a closed gate, while a concentrated owner can fund valuable independent tests. Distribution of returns and control of learning are related questions that need separate evidence. The final tax chapter can refer back to this distinction without inferring an optimal rate from it.

## The comparative centerpiece: Who can keep the next entrant possible?

Follow the same fictional small team through three possible routes to computing and evaluation. It is an institutional comparison, not an invented visit, interview, or outcome. Place actual dated arrangements beside each route, then ask what remains untested.

**European Union.** Start with a real application rule rather than a general reputation for regulation. EuroHPC's Playground call provides an eligibility and technical assessment route for SMEs and startups; its published process is first-come rather than competitively ranked and warns that capacity may be unavailable. Its broader Industrial Innovation access modes offer eligible AI SMEs free access for innovation. These are concrete possible entry routes, not evidence that Europe has solved frontier-model entry. Ask whether allocated computing leads to an independently developed product, access to customers, and a usable performance record. A European national champion behind an equally closed interface would not answer that question. [Playground call](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/playground-access-ai-factories_en); [access modes](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/ai-factories/ai-factories-access-modes_en).

**United States.** Give the counterevidence equal narrative force. The July 2025 AI Action Plan combines deregulation with support for open-weight models and wider computing access. NSF announced a NAIRR operations center in September 2026. The FTC's January 2025 inquiry report examined potential partnership lock-in. These records contradict a categorical assertion that America has no tools or capacity to intervene. They do not prove that its measures deliver independent entry. The conditional concern is that protecting scale, investment, or strategic leadership could also protect a private permission structure. Evaluate the actual access terms, enforcement, capital supply, and ability to switch, rather than treating deregulation as an outcome measure. [AI Action Plan](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf), printed pp. 3–5; [NAIRR announcement](https://www.nsf.gov/cise/updates/nsf-establishes-operations-center-national-artificial); [FTC study and dated scope](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/ftc-staff-report-ai-partnerships-investments-6b-study).

**China.** Put support and restriction in the same account. The State Council's August 2025 AI Plus policy calls for agent services, shared computing, open-source development, employment-related adaptation, and patient capital. It is a policy statement, not an achieved outcome. The 2023 generative-AI measures combine support for infrastructure and innovation with political content requirements for covered public services. Commercial experimentation could therefore widen in some domains while permitted public conclusions remain constrained. The question is whether state capacity opens independently usable routes or makes official permission the common gate. Do not infer that every Chinese research activity falls under rules expressly scoped to public-facing services. [Original AI Plus policy](https://www.nda.gov.cn/sjj/swdt/xwfb/0826/20250826173046592282838_mobile.html), Section II and Section III (9)–(13); [original interim measures](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm), Articles 2, 4–6.

Avoid a simple ranking by commitment to property. EU law expressly protects property and intellectual property while permitting regulated use under stated conditions. The question is the content of particular exclusion rights, duties, and remedies, alongside reliable enforcement. Also distinguish the EU's institutions from member-state governments; Europe is not one fiscal or political decision-maker. [EU Charter Article 17](https://fra.europa.eu/en/eu-charter/charter/article/17-right-property).

## What robustness would mean

Define it before predicting who possesses it. Here, long-run robustness means the ability to discover and adopt workable alternatives after technological, commercial, or policy failure, at acceptable cost. This is a proposed decision-oriented criterion, not a computed comparison budget, established country index, or forecast of national growth.

Assess the same questions in all three settings: Can an unaffiliated team obtain resources and customers? Can it preserve and transfer useful records? Can it test a different design with independent outcome checks? Can it publish an inconvenient finding and challenge exclusion? Can useful scale survive the remedy? What happens when the public evaluator is wrong? Examine resource costs, implementation delay, research incentives, privacy, security, access quality, and displaced private tests.

The plausible long-run claim is conditional. A society that sustains several effective routes to consequential testing could adapt better when its leading arrangement fails. A rival system might develop the first frontier capability sooner yet make alternatives harder to develop later. The reverse is also possible: an expensive openness regime might suppress entry or prevent the investment it needs. These are scenarios to compare, not a conclusion that the EU or China will outperform the US.
