## Editorial verdict

There is a coherent book here. Its strongest thesis is that success changes the opportunities through which an economy can learn about contribution and alternatives. Its proposed original contribution is the comparison-budget formulation, the information/precision bound tied to it, and the economic attribution interpretation. The book was weakening that claim by repeatedly explaining what examples did not prove, treating the better-known phenomenon of incomplete learning as though it were the whole discovery, and postponing its policy reasoning behind research-procedure lists.

The revision states the thesis early, introduces the author's paper before referring to its result, gives applications distinct jobs, and shortens repeated research instructions in the final chapter. The conclusion is a program for preserving discovery, correcting demonstrated external costs and financing productive access. Those recommendations use the model alongside evidence and an explicit objective; they are not a tax schedule deduced from an impossibility theorem.

## The thesis on the page

Success can close the comparisons needed to explain success. A reward can finance real improvement while also changing whose alternative gets a meaningful trial. The comparison budget accumulates conditional allocation chance outside the current favorite. In the specified models it bounds information about productive input effects. Under the additional lifetime and equivalence/separation conditions, one indefinitely extended reward history cannot support a universally consistent attribution procedure.

Prediction may remain useful and forecasts may merge. Informative outcome checks, private tests and other independent measurements count. The empirical question concerns the actual observation and allocation process. No concentration chart establishes an economy's finite lifetime budget.

## How the conclusion follows

1. The model distinguishes another transaction from additional information about contribution.
2. Historical and contemporary cases identify institutions controlling access to customers, inputs, development and evaluation. They motivate applications; they do not establish the theorem's infinite-horizon conditions for named firms.
3. Preserving useful independent discovery is the stated public objective. That objective has to be assessed alongside productive scale, security, privacy and resource costs.
4. When an indispensable gate threatens irreversible closure of a useful development route, access duties address that obstruction before the missing rival becomes untestable.
5. When conduct prevents valuable learning and leaves an unreplaced loss with others, corrective charges can address the external cost. Causation, a consequential decision, replacement and private responses determine the case.
6. Where a workable attempt lacks financing, opening the gate is insufficient. Shared capabilities and broader ownership need resources. Information benefits beyond the payer give a further financing rationale; the public program still needs accountable selection and useful results.
7. Capital income and inheritance taxation are the book's proposed means of helping finance those capabilities and widening ownership. Their relative merits and rates depend on behavior, administration and effects on investment. Uncertain desert alone does not select them.
8. The author's subsequent steering adds a firm universal-basic-income position. Where private arrangements displace a shared capacity for development and comparison, the public should retain an equal basic claim on part of the returns. A universal payment avoids conditioning entitlement on proving a counterfactual career. This shared-entitlement principle supplies the normative bridge to universality; uncertainty alone does not prove equal individual losses. A regular unconditional payment, progressive financing and broadly held capital are proposed alongside continuing access duties. It is not a license to buy permanent exclusion or a replacement for useful public services.

The argument is forceful when these steps stay visible. 'We cannot measure merit, therefore redistribute' would skip the public objective and the causal/financing argument. 'The incumbent is large, therefore its comparison budget is finite' would misapply the model. Neither inference is introduced by the revision.

## Kauffman and the level of ambition

The primary 2003 *Edge* account quotes Kauffman considering sustainable expansion of the adjacent possible as a candidate general tendency. It supplies a concise precedent for making a bold conjecture while identifying the process it describes. The final chapter now connects that question to an economy's capacity to develop and test possibilities. It does not attribute a thermodynamic law, the user's entire extinction/contagion formulation, or an accepted scientific consensus to *At Home in the Universe*. The 2022 fourth-law preprint was identified but no result from it is imported into the book.

A useful economic proposition is that productive capacity and the capacity to judge alternatives can grow apart when shared gateways make independent development and evaluation hard to sustain. The allocation theorem establishes a defined information constraint under its conditions. The wider institutional proposition remains an argument to test in actual markets. Keeping those levels clear permits confident prose without turning a conditional result into a universal law.
