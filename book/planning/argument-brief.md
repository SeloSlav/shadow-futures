# Shadow Futures Argument Brief

Martin Erlic   16 October 2026

A song called Lockdown finished first in one MusicLab world and fortieth in another. The recording was the same, competing against the same songs. What changed was the sequence of listeners and the popularity information they encountered. Salganik, Dodds, and Watts created the parallel markets; the rankings are also described in Salganik's own account. Shadow Futures begins with that documented result and asks what we can learn about success when ordinary economic life gives us only one history (Salganik, Dodds, and Watts, 2006; Salganik, Bit by Bit).

### The comparison budget

The comparison budget accumulates the modeled chance that an allocation goes outside the current favorite. The favorite can change. The object describes conditional opportunities, rather than realized outsider wins, independent trials, firm counts, or market shares. Netflix artwork experiments, randomized Yahoo logs, and adaptive recruitment calls carry the explanation alongside brief anonymous probability examples.

In my specified allocation model, that budget places an upper bound on the information the award record supplies about productive input effects. A large budget still requires informative differences between alternatives. A small chance repeated indefinitely can accumulate without limit. Concentration or a low current chance alone therefore does not establish the strongest result. The relevant question is how much comparison the whole allocation process preserves (Erlic, v5, Sections 2 and 3).

### Shadow futures

A shadow future is another feasible allocation history with the same starting conditions, underlying input effects, and response rules. Different early events can induce different later investment under unchanged rules. An intervention changes an input or policy. MusicLab supplies an observed parallel-market case; hypothetical histories require a specified model and external validation.

The strongest result requires common rules, the same possible next winners under each explanation, controlled differences between their predictions, and a finite lifetime comparison budget. Under those conditions, there is no single method whose contribution estimates become reliably accurate under every candidate explanation as one history grows. I call the unresolved attribution contribution uncertainty. Work can still matter. Extra outcome measurements carrying information about its effect must be counted separately. Forecasts can nevertheless converge, using Blackwell and Dubins's classical result (1962), while the attribution limit remains (Erlic, v5, Section 3 and Appendix B).

### What is new

The developmental spine is explicit: success can close the comparisons needed to explain success. The research paper is introduced by title and SSRN location before its result is discussed. The proposed original contribution is the specified allocation quantity, its information/precision bound, and its attribution interpretation; the broad phenomena of path dependence and incomplete learning are credited to predecessors. The chapter-by-chapter argument and bounded novelty assessment are recorded in `developmental-review-2026-10-18.md`.

I propose an economic information bound connecting the comparison budget to what reward histories reveal about the effects of work, quality, and investment. It limits precision in a finite record and, under further conditions, reliable attribution as history extends indefinitely. Alchian (1950) already explained profit-based selection under uncertainty. Increasing returns and cumulative advantage are established ideas (Arthur, 1989; Merton, 1968). Estimation failure in a reinforced urn predates this paper (Le Goff and Soulier, 2017), as does accurate prediction alongside inconsistent parameter estimation (Zhang, 2004). The contribution is this defined object, bound, and economic interpretation. Its probability foundations are classical; priority requires wider review.

The introduction now makes the descriptive distinction early. Preferential attachment and network growth explain a pattern of accumulated connections (Barabási and Albert, 1999); they do not measure the opportunities left to identify productive contribution. A growing, useful service can retain informative trials or foreclose them. The comparison budget addresses what its allocation process lets observers learn, before moral judgments about deserving. The historical and policy thread concerns the reach of shared selection gates, the development routes they can close, and potentially unpriced losses of useful evidence. Act before an irreversible closure makes the rival untestable. Keep this institutional case distinct from a measured economy-wide prevalence estimate or a universal ranking of information harms above price harms.

### Scale and the reach of a judgment

A market can grow while the number of independent places to try again shrinks. Smith's market extent, old guild restrictions, industrial steel, Bell's network, and platform ratings supply the historical thread. Wider reach can expand opportunity while synchronizing the judgments that allocate it. The proposed qualitative change concerns the disappearance of independent routes to evaluation, not size alone. It is a historical hypothesis requiring evidence, not a transition proved by v5. Anderson's More Is Different supplies an explicitly credited analogy.

Make the development barrier explicit: an entrant viable at a mature network scale may have no feasible path to that scale under existing access and financing arrangements. Control of those routes can prolong the period over which returns compound without a credible challenge. Technical AI scaling laws alone do not prove permanent exclusion. The historical, AI, competition and final chapters now connect productive scale, development routes, evidence and durable capital advantage. Chapter 5 answers Thiel's argument that durable monopoly profits finance innovation: acknowledge his long-term rationale and anticipated succession of innovators, then examine whether incumbent control prevents successors from acquiring a development route. Evaluate both visible investment and foregone independent learning.

### AI and work

AI makes the distinction between activity and comparison consequential. Frontier training and deployment depend on compute, infrastructure, and distribution. Inference means running a trained model to produce an output. A growing volume of outputs can reveal performance on encountered tasks without independently testing alternative suppliers, training strategies, or economic arrangements. AI can also lower the cost of those tests. Whether it expands the comparison budget is an empirical and institutional question. The work chapter pairs observed productivity gains in customer support with METR's account of developers avoiding tasks that might be assigned to a no-AI condition (Brynjolfsson, Li, and Raymond, 2025; METR, 2026).

Chapters 8 and 9 now ask who owns an agent workforce and the assets it helps build. Agent activity, productive capability, capital accumulation, and independent authority over useful tests are distinct. Carlini's reported compiler experiment supplies a production case; Korinek and Suh supply prior automation/capital scenario analysis. The additional comparative hypothesis is that public action preserving workable independent entry could sustain more competition than protection of a particular private bottleneck. EU, US and Chinese access arrangements will be assessed under the same questions, including public control of judgment. State capacity alone supplies no ranking of openness or long-run resilience. The revision is specified in `agents-capital-and-three-economies.md`; the comparative section and Chapters 11–12 links are now integrated and reviewed for their recorded claims.

### Antitrust

An access rule or merger can change which alternatives receive interpretable trials. This supplies a possible informational rationale for intervention alongside established price, quality, and innovation concerns. I use epistemic monopoly for control over relevant comparison production, explicitly crediting Meghani's prior use of the term (2021). An intervention must improve a consequential decision enough to justify its costs. Internal experiments may preserve learning, and independent routes may be expensive. This economic argument does not itself establish legal liability or remove existing statutory requirements (Erlic, v5, Sections 4 and 5).

A sole AI provider is not automatically a total epistemic monopoly. The explicit OpenAI hypothetical adds control over indispensable development, distribution, and evaluation routes. I favor a rebuttable presumption for workable independent testing access where credible, hard-to-reverse foreclosure threatens valuable comparison. Interfaces, interoperability, appropriate knowledge access, portability, and independent appeal can preserve private ownership while limiting control over others' discovery. Arrow and Fisher supply the option-preservation antecedent; Bell and the DMA supply concrete boundary obligations. The burden, scope, costs, and legal authority require specification.

### Taxation

The policy conclusion now explicitly includes universal basic income. It combines access duties, correction of demonstrated information externalities, financing independent attempts and wider ownership, and an unconditional universal social dividend. The dividend rests on an equal public claim where private arrangements displace a shared capacity to develop and test alternatives. No recipient must establish the counterfactual enterprise that might have existed. Universality concerns the payment; progressive taxation and broadly held capital finance it. It remains distinct from a conduct-based damages charge, from project grants requiring qualification, and from the access duties needed to keep discovery possible. Paine, Van Parijs and Alaska's public fund supply intellectual and institutional precedents; they are not credited with the comparison-budget result. No inference from a few hyperscalers to a finite civilization-wide budget is made.

There are two arguments. First, economies that generate indistinguishable observable histories can attribute different contributions to the same reward. A rule using those histories cannot divide every reward exactly into earned income and positional advantage in both economies. This separate hidden-position argument does not invalidate optimal taxation (Erlic, v5, Section 5 and Appendix E; Piketty, Saez, and Stantcheva, 2014; Rothschild and Scheuer, 2016). Second, conduct removing informative trials may impose an external cost. A corrective tax needs evidence of that causal link, a valuable affected decision, private incentives that miss part of the social cost, and an instrument worth its cost. Arrow helps supply the additional welfare foundation (1962, 1963).

The political conclusion now commits to protecting workable independent evaluation routes before credible lasting foreclosure, correcting demonstrated external losses, and using capital income and inheritance taxation to finance shared capabilities and broader ownership. The public objective is independent discovery and meaningful entry. It does not depend on proving individual taxpayers undeserving, and the theorem does not select a tax rate or instrument. Scheuer and Slemrod (2021) supply a published review of tax design and behavioral responses. Wider ownership, income security, access and information production remain distinct aims. Public evaluators require competing methods, transparent admission and appeal. Present the prescriptions through concrete choices and consequences, rather than repeated declarations of the author's preferences.

### The book

The narrative returns to Lockdown and follows documented experiments, economic history, AI research, labor studies, and policy records. There is no recurring fictional council or invented supplier storyline. The author's intellectual journey and work supply a personal thread: counterfactual regret and complexity reading in the introduction; an unnamed olive-oil venture beside Akerlof; crypto gains, losses and missed Bitcoin beside risk; cloud work beside deployment; local relationships in the epilogue. These experiences explain the stakes and motivation without proving the theorem or a policy result. The final chapter assembles the access, precaution, corrective-tax and broader-ownership prescriptions; the epilogue returns to Lockdown and concludes that an economy should reward its winners without giving them ownership of everyone else's chance. The original SSRN remains the only theory self-citation. One published 2016 Medium essay documents the author's intellectual history.
