# Shadow Futures Book Outline

Martin Erlic   20 October 2026

An 80000 word popular economics book about what reinforced markets can reveal about contribution and how institutions can preserve useful comparison. The argument moves from one song's contrasting outcomes to the choices governing AI, work, competition, and taxation. The book uses published research and public records. Its first draft requires no interviews.

## 2 The comparison budget

Explain a short anonymous sequence of allocation probabilities in ordinary words, then use Netflix, Yahoo, and adaptive recruitment to examine actual observation designs. Separate modeled chance, realized wins, independent trials, and informative input contrasts.

Explain why mostly repeating the favored choice may supply little information about input effects, even while the service improves and transactions multiply. Define the particular information question before describing its scarcity. A high comparison budget is an opportunity for information, not a guarantee of identification.

Give two endings. In one, a persistent chance remains and comparison continues accumulating. In the other, chances diminish fast enough for their lifetime total to be finite. Describe strong reinforcement as one sufficient setting, crediting the earlier reinforced-process literature. Close with the cost of allocating some contracts to less certain suppliers.

V5 citations: Bar-Yam (2016); Pemantle (2007); Oliveira (2009); Gottfried and Grosskinsky (2024); Kasy and Sautmann (2021). V5 Sections 2 and 3 and Appendix D.

## 3 Shadow futures

Replay the procurement example with the same underlying parameters, starting state, and response rules, but different early awards. Let later behavior change only as the unchanged rules prescribe. Separate these histories from an intervention that changes an input or a policy. Return briefly to MusicLab as an observed analogue of parallel histories, not a test of the theorem.

Use Le Goff and Soulier's ant-fork experiment as a concrete predecessor: a continued reinforced path differs from repeated finite trials. John Snow's 1854 comparison of intermingled London water-company customers supplies a historical counterweight: useful causal variation can exist within one observed history. Do not identify either case with a measured comparison budget or an empirical proof of the lifetime result.

Explain the finite-budget result in prose and preserve its conditions. Candidate explanations must use the common recorded experiment, retain the same possible next recipients, and differ in a way controlled by remaining comparison. Additional informative outcomes belong in the experiment. The conclusion is a failure of universal consistent recovery, not identical probabilities, total ignorance, or a guarantee that every observed market is permanently unknowable.

Use Blackwell and Dubins to explain how forecasts can converge while attribution remains unresolved in v5's setting. Credit Le Goff and Soulier and Zhang for prior estimation and prediction results. A single grouped technical endnote credits Kakutani, Kabanov and colleagues, Jacod and Shiryaev, Gabriyelyan, and Le Cam and Yang. Close with what an independent, informative replication could add.

V5 citations: Blackwell and Dubins (1962); Le Goff and Soulier (2017); Zhang (2004); Kakutani (1948); Kabanov, Liptser, and Shiryaev (1977); Jacod and Shiryaev (2003); Gabriyelyan (2011); Le Cam and Yang (2000). V5 Appendices A to E.

## The earlier work on information and uncertainty

### Alchian in 1950

Uncertainty, Evolution, and Economic Theory is the closest title match to the remembered 1950s paper. Alchian explains how realized profits can select behavior even without individually optimal foresight. Shadow Futures asks what a resulting history can reveal about productive contributions when past rewards alter later opportunities. This is a further attribution question, not a general refutation of market selection. Alchian is an added reference, absent from v5's 24-item bibliography.

### Blackwell in 1953

Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments gives a decision-based way to compare experiments. Roughly, one experiment is at least as informative as another when it permits at least as good a decision across the theorem's decision problems, with a corresponding statistical transformation characterization. This is a foundation for valuing preserved comparison, not a theorem that more observations always justify their cost. It is an addition to the book's bibliography, not one of v5's 24 references.

### Blackwell and Dubins in 1962

Merging of Opinions with Increasing Information is the direct classical source cited in v5. Under its absolute-continuity condition, conditional predictions merge as information accumulates. It does not say everyone converges to truth or that causal effects become identifiable. V5 combines this result with its particular single-history allocation limit.

### Arrow in 1962

Economic Welfare and the Allocation of Resources for Invention studies knowledge production under uncertainty and problems of appropriating its benefits. It supports the welfare discussion about private incentives and useful information. It does not establish the comparison budget or price its depletion. This is an added book reference.

### Arrow in 1963

Uncertainty and the Welfare Economics of Medical Care examines how uncertainty about illness and treatment, information differences, and risk-bearing shape medical institutions. This is another uncertainty classic from the period. It is a comparison for the institutional argument, not evidence that healthcare satisfies v5's theorem. It is an added book reference.

### The wider lineage

Knight's Risk, Uncertainty and Profit is from 1921, outside the 1950s and 1960s. Hayek's competition-as-discovery lecture dates to 1968; v5 cites the 2002 translation. Merton's Matthew effect is from 1968. Kakutani's mathematical predecessor is from 1948. Akerlof's lemons paper is from 1970. These are different contributions and should not be compressed into a claim that uncertainty or market learning was previously overlooked.

All 24 references in the canonical October 2026 v5 are retained below and assigned to chapters in research/v5_reference_register.json. Relevant antecedents receive author-date attribution in the explanatory text or a specific endnote. Grouped notes credit mathematical sources without introducing equations. Before a chapter's factual or novelty claim is finalized, check the relevant full source and add its page or section to the working citation record. Identify unpublished Erlic extensions as unpublished drafts and state their additional assumptions.
